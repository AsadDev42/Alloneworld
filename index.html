About

The word ‘alone’ comes from separating and joining together the two words, ‘all’ and ‘one’.

alloneworld is a psychosocial community bringing people together in safe spaces to explore what it means to be human and how to foster a sense of belonging in an increasingly oppressive, alienating and frantic world.

INTERDISCIPLINARY PRACTICE

Systemic Human Exploration (S.H.E.)

Systemic Human Exploration is an interdisciplinary and creative approach for addressing human problems in groups through combined psychosocial education, embodied exploration, and meaningful exchange.

Engaging with the fundamental question of what it means to be human in an increasingly troubled world, S.H.E. offers ways to effectively support people from all walks of life in their daily struggles, dilemmas, and transitions—whether facing life upheavals, struggling with inertia, or feeling lost.

Explore S.H.E. Methodology

Reflective Group Discussions

Exploring multi-personal networks and relational matrixes using clear, jargon-free language to make complex psychological dynamics inviting.

Embodied & Sensory Exploration

Engaging somatic mindfulness, sensory presence, and body-oriented exploration beyond purely verbal analysis.

Artistic & Creative Expression

Harnessing music, creative writing, visual symbols, and play to unlock imagination and process daunting human conflicts.

Group-Based Experiential Learning

Fostering mutual resonance where therapeutic change and shared discovery emerge naturally through intersubjective connection.

Reem Shelhi

Anglo-Arab Contemporary Psychotherapist, Group Analyst & Clinical Supervisor

“In founding alloneworld.org, my aim is to adopt innovative, engaging, and accessible methods of drawing people together, sharing knowledge, and facilitating difficult and controversial conversations on a wide range of issues related to the human condition, the human situation, and opportunities for change.”

My intersectional identity and socio-political experiences have deeply influenced my work on matters of race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality, and neurodiversity which are central to my practice. Existential concerns connected with loss, grief, and the search for meaning and belonging animate my thinking and professional explorations.
